Privacy

A tier states who can read your data

Evidence decides the tier, and a seller cannot pick one. You choose the tier you need in advance, and the key stays on your machine until a seller meets it.

Tiers

TierMachineDesigned to protect againstSuitable data
T0Your own machineThird partiesAny
T3-COrdinary GPU serverStrangers on the networkPublic or synthetic data

On a T3-C seller, the machine's owner can read a job. Keep personal data on your own machine.

Floors

Your floor is the strictest of three things: your global policy, the data's label and this request. It is enforced at key release, so if a job were ever routed to a weaker seller, that seller would still hold only ciphertext.

Data labelWhere it can go
local_onlyThis machine only
secretThis machine
personalThis machine
synthetic, publicAny seller

No agent API changes a label. An adapter inherits the label of its training data. Beetle never lowers a floor automatically.

Evidence

Each job's evidence is bound to that job, that specification and that pair of one-time keys. Your machine verifies it locally, so no central service decides for you.

Two layers of encryption

The outer layer is iroh's QUIC and TLS 1.3, which ends at the host and carries traffic. The inner layer is HPKE sealed to the environment's one-time key, in fixed-size frames. Relays, storage and the chain only ever see ciphertext.

Proof card

Every connection ends with a card with two separate lamps: one for the way there, one for the other machine. The client writes the wording. A seller supplies no text.

Agents

Limits live in a local daemon, outside the agent. An agent works with a token that can spend within the policy. It sees dataset names and never paths, and it has no call to raise a limit, lower a floor or change a label.
