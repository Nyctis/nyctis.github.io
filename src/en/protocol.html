Why Solana, and why not our own chain

One million jobs a day is under 100 transactions per second, which an existing chain can carry. A new chain would add a bridge for USDC and a validator set to run, with nothing to gain. Per-token traffic stays off chain in payment channels.

Solana gives us native USDC, fast confirmation, low fees, no single sequencer, and a payment-channel program from the Solana Foundation. The full reasoning

The sandbox has no network, so everything it sees was carried in by the host. A hash mismatch is therefore never counted against the buyer, because a seller could cause it on purpose.

Paying for inference

Inference does not touch the Beetle program. The buyer opens a Solana Foundation payment channel with a one-time session key and deposits a budget. Cumulative vouchers are signed in small steps off chain. At the end the seller settles with the last voucher and the balance returns to the buyer.

  • A buyer can overpay by at most one step plus the current input. A seller can work unpaid for at most one step.
  • If the seller vanishes, the buyer closes the channel and recovers the rest.
  • If the buyer vanishes, the seller settles with the vouchers it holds.
  • Usage is the smaller of the buyer's count and the seller's count. The session stops if the two drift apart.

How two machines find each other

A contract cannot send packets, so the chain replaces a coordination server and nothing more. Each side writes its candidate addresses, sealed to the other, then both dial at the same moment. Addresses stay sealed on chain, so only the two parties can read them.

With relays off, the two machines connect directly. If a link cannot form, Beetle moves to the next seller, up to three, and never goes below your floor.
